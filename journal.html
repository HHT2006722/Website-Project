Why the Site Has an English Version

21 October 2026 · Build notes

My teacher doesn't read Chinese. A Chinese-only site means the person marking it can't read the content — everything written here would count for nothing.

So the root is English, the Chinese version sits under zh/, and both share the images and scripts. The last nav item switches language, each page declares three hreflang links, and there are two feeds. I didn't swap text with JavaScript on one page: search engines would see only one language, and if the script failed the site would be monolingual again.

The cost is that every Chinese sentence needs an English twin. Renaming two photos today touched twelve files and eighteen references. The code comments are two lines now, Chinese then English — but they're stripped when the stylesheet is inlined, because shipping the same explanation twelve times is pointless.

Two Photos Left, and It Still Paged to a Third

21 October 2026 · Build notes

Filtered to "Through glass", two photos left. Opened the lightbox, pressed Next, and landed on a third — one that had just been filtered out. The counter still said "of 6".

Every part was right on its own: the filter toggles visibility, the lightbox swaps the image. The fault sat between them — the list it cycled through was built from all six when the page loaded, and nothing told it only two were left. The fix is to recompute the visible order when the lightbox opens and cycle within that group.

You can't find this by reading code. You have to walk the real path: filter, open, step, close.

Three Sizes for One Photo

19 October 2026 · Build notes

An original is one to three hundred KB; the thumbnail on the page is about 330 pixels wide. A small square used to cost you the whole file.

So each photo is exported at three widths (480 / 960 / 1600) and the page only says how wide the slot is, letting the browser choose. Adding a 768 step later brought all six from 585 KB down to 436 KB. I broke that markup once: the browser recognised only two widths and took the larger one, and the first screen went from 3.2 s to 5.1 s. That kind of mistake raises no error — it just costs bandwidth.

Every image also carries its real width and height, so nothing jumps while loading. The grid fits as many 220px columns as it can: one on a phone, three on a desktop, without a single media query.

Enlarging a Photo Without a Mouse

19 October 2026 · Build notes

The first enlarged view was a box and an overlay I wrote myself: click to open, click to close. Fine with a mouse, impossible with a keyboard.

Switching to the browser's dialog gave Esc, focus containment and an inert background for free. Two things still had to be added by hand: focus goes to Close when it opens, and back to the photo you are actually looking at when it closes — not the one you first opened.

I also fixed two things nobody could see: in dark mode the selected filter button and the checklist numbers put white on pale orange, a ratio of 2.4. They now use the darker orange: 4.69.

The Form I Rewrote Three Times

19 October 2026 · Build notes

The first version checked for an @. a@b, test@ and @x.com all passed, and it thanked me for the message.

The second used a pattern to check the shape, tied each error to its field and told screen readers which field was invalid. The third changed the attitude: this is a static site and the form cannot store anything. Instead of claiming it received the message, it says plainly that Send opens your mail app — and that if nothing opens, you can write to the address, which is printed on the page.

Glass, Weak Light, and Waiting

19 October 2026 · Shooting notes

All six were shot indoors, through glass or fencing. There are only three problems.

Glass. Put the lens against it: the closer it is, the softer the fingerprints and scratches. Block the side of the lens with a jacket — reflections mostly come from the side or from behind. No flash: it fires back as a sheet of white and the animals flinch.

Weak light. Light comes from above, so a raised head puts the eyes in shadow. Tap to focus on the eyes, drag the exposure down — better dark than blurred. Skip digital zoom; walk closer instead.

Waiting. Whether the frame works depends on whether the animal looks at the lens. I waited fifteen minutes for a spot of light to reach one animal's back, and shot seven or eight frames that were no good.

Swapping Out the Six Illustrations

19 October 2026 · Build notes

The first six images on this site were illustrations, not mine. One of them still carried the original artist's signature in the corner. I saw it and looked away.

It didn't hold. Even labelled as a copy, I couldn't explain why anything inside those pictures was the way it was — the whole site was propping up a claim that wasn't true. So I deleted all six and replaced them with my own photographs.

The photographs are less pretty. Glass in the way, flat overhead light, animals that don't cooperate. So nothing under a photo claims it is good; each one says what I gave up.
